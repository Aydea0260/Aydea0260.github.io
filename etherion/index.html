The system

Editorial pipeline

Etherion's editorial work runs through seven stages, each executed by several of its ~28 AI subagents.

  1. Discover & acquireSpot trends and openings in science fiction and fantasy; receive and evaluate manuscripts; assess originality, audience and editorial & commercial potential; find stories that fit the imprint.
  2. Concept developmentTurn an idea into a full editorial proposal: genre, subgenre, tone, premise, central conflict, reader promises, themes; decide standalone, series or shared universe.
  3. WorldbuildingDesign geography, territories and planets; ecosystems and climates; cultures, languages, religions and traditions; political and economic structures; species and creatures; and the rules that govern the universe.
  4. Magic & speculative scienceSet the origin, users, rules, limits, costs and consequences of magic; design future or alternative technologies and check their internal logic; keep systems consistent and conflict intact.
  5. Characters & narrative architectureBuild protagonists, antagonists and secondary characters with motives, fears and contradictions; design structure, pacing, mysteries, reveals and the climax; seed elements for later books.
  6. Saga development & canonDesign the saga arc and each book's purpose; distribute reveals across volumes; keep a canon bible with characters, species, places, timeline and rules; protect continuity in adaptations.
  7. Art, production, metadata & publishingDefine the visual language and covers; prepare interiors, print and digital files, colour profiles and proofs; write titles, descriptions, categories and keywords; publish through Scriba's infrastructure.

An Etherion editorial director coordinates each work and assigns the subagents. The subagents prepare and recommend; the critical decisions are approved by a person responsible for the project.

Transmedia

Adaptations

A selected work can become an audiobook, a narrative podcast, a film or TV project, animation or anime, a comic or graphic novel, a game or interactive experience, music, physical products or live experiences — produced with Great Studios.

  1. Book & canon approved
  2. Assess the new medium
  3. Adaptation bible
  4. Script & concept design
  5. Production with Great Studios
  6. Continuity control
  7. Launch & commercialisation

Every adaptation must have:

  • A defined objective and audience
  • Clearly established rights
  • The universe bible
  • A list of elements that must be preserved
  • Changes authorised for the new format
  • A continuity review
  • Final human approval
